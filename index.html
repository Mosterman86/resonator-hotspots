The Resonator Guitar

The resonator guitar — known by the trade name Dobro — is one of the most ingenious acoustic innovations of the twentieth century. Unlike conventional guitars, which project sound through a hollow wooden body, the resonator uses a spun aluminum cone to convert string vibration into far greater volume.

The instrument originated in the 1920s from a collaboration between Slovak-born instrument maker John Dopyera and steel guitarist George Beauchamp, who needed a guitar loud enough to carry over brass and wind instruments in a dance orchestra. Rather than rely on electronic amplification, Dopyera designed a purely mechanical solution.

Dopyera and Beauchamp co-founded the National String Instrument Corporation in 1927 to manufacture the first resonator guitars. Dopyera later departed National under disputed circumstances—sources place his exit in either 1928 or 1929—and founded the Dobro Manufacturing Company with his brothers and business partner Vic Smith. The name is a contraction of Dopyera Brothers that also translates as “good” in Slovak and other Slavic languages.

The instrument appealed immediately to Hawaiian, blues, country, and bluegrass musicians. Three resonator types emerged: the tricone (National's original design), the biscuit bridge single-cone (favored in blues), and the spider bridge single-cone (the sound most associated with bluegrass).

This interactive is a proof of concept developed by Wolfsonian Digital Labs in preparation for a forthcoming exhibition focused on the resonator guitar — its invention, its cultural reach across blues, bluegrass, Hawaiian, and country music, and its place within the broader history of acoustic instrument design.

The Wolfsonian–FIU's collection spans designed objects at the intersection of technology, culture, and persuasion. This tool explores how digital interactives can extend that interpretive work beyond the gallery wall, making material culture accessible and legible to a wide audience.

Content, annotations, and audio are preliminary and subject to revision as the exhibition develops.

This tool is an annotated interactive diagram. Here is how to navigate it:

  • Hover or tap any glowing dot on the guitar to reveal the name and description of that component.
  • Click or tap again to lock the panel open so you can read without holding your cursor in place.
  • Click anywhere else on the guitar stage to dismiss and return to the idle state.
  • The Overview panel on the left provides background on the instrument's history and invention.
  • Use the Listen to Sample button to hear a short recording of a resonator guitar, with a fade in and fade out.

On mobile, use the Overview and Component Detail tabs below the guitar to switch between panels.
